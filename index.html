01 — THE IDEA

A frozen VLA. A changing body.

Adapt the action to the robot's current capability.

A joint lock changes the relation between commands and motion, even when the goal remains the same. CAPABLE builds an actuator-aware representation from execution feedback and uses it to correct the VLA's six arm action dimensions. The gripper command passes through unchanged.

Read the paper abstract +

Vision-language-action policies are trained on a particular robot embodiment and may fail when an actuator no longer realizes the motion that the policy commands. CAPABLE adapts a frozen VLA to impaired actuation without fault labels, affected-joint identifiers, or fault-specific demonstrations. It represents physical capability through each joint’s command–response history, grounds the estimate in the live Jacobian, and combines joint representations with shared temporal encoding and cross-joint attention. Self-supervised physical prediction shapes a latent that conditions a bounded residual action. Across 28 LIBERO tasks, CAPABLE raises success on a globally unseen joint lock from 24.8% to 59.3%, versus 41.9% for a matched global-history residual baseline. On a physical Franka Panda with software-enforced locks, it succeeds in 24/30 trials versus 13/30 for the baseline.

01 / OBSERVE

What was commanded? What actually moved?

A shared temporal encoder processes a 16-step command–response history for each of seven joints. Sharing the encoder avoids a fixed learned joint identity while preserving each joint's measured behavior.

Evaluation protocol +

Three independently initialized seeds, 1.4 million environment steps each. The main pool has 28 LIBERO tasks, and success is task-balanced. Six additional leave-one-actuator-out splits use a common eight-task subset. Cross-fault evaluations reuse lock-trained checkpoints without retraining.
