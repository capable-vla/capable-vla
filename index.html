CAPABLE: Capability-Aware Policy Adaptation via Behavioral Latent Encoding

When a robot joint stops responding as expected, CAPABLE uses execution feedback to adapt a frozen vision-language-action policy—without fault labels or fault-specific demonstrations.

Paper abstract

At a glance

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are trained on a particular robot embodiment and may fail when an actuator no longer realizes the motion that the policy commands. We present CAPABLE, a capability-aware residual reinforcement-learning method that adapts a frozen VLA to impaired actuation without fault labels, affected-joint identifiers, or fault-specific demonstrations. It represents physical capability through each joint’s observed command–response history, grounds the estimate in the live Jacobian, and combines the joint representations using shared temporal encoding and cross-joint self-attention. Self-supervised physical prediction shapes the resulting latent, which conditions a bounded residual action. On 28 LIBERO tasks, CAPABLE increases success under a globally unseen joint lock from 24.8% to 59.3%, compared with 41.9% for a parameter-matched global-history residual baseline, while largely retaining healthy performance. The advantage extends across six independently held-out actuators and to a physical Franka Panda under software-enforced joint locks, where CAPABLE reaches 24/30 successful trials versus 13/30 for the matched baseline.

The problem

The task is understood. The body has changed.

A vision-language-action policy can choose a sensible action yet fail when an actuator cannot produce the commanded motion. CAPABLE estimates the robot's current physical capability from what each joint was told to do and how it actually moved, then corrects the frozen policy's arm action.

The approach

Learn what each joint can do, then adjust the action

CAPABLE adds a bounded residual controller to a frozen OpenVLA-OFT policy. It corrects the six arm action dimensions while preserving the VLA's gripper command. The capability representation is updated from observed execution at every control step.

01

Command–response history

A shared temporal encoder processes a 16-step history for each of the robot's seven joints. It compares commands with realized joint and end-effector motion without learned joint identities.

02

Physically grounded capability

The live Jacobian grounds each joint's history in its current contribution to end-effector motion. Cross-joint attention combines the seven representations into one capability latent.

03

Residual reinforcement learning

Self-supervised motion prediction shapes the latent, which conditions a SAC residual actor. The bounded correction is added to the frozen VLA's nominal arm action.

Across held-out actuators

In six separately trained leave-one-actuator-out splits on a common eight-task subset, CAPABLE exceeds Global-history SAC on every held-out joint. Mean success is 67.0% versus 42.4%. This eight-task test is separate from the main 28-task result.

Across fault types

Training uses persistent locks only. On unseen j₂, CAPABLE exceeds the matched baseline by +12.2 points under damping, +12.4 under friction, and +10.9 under a late-onset lock. The matched baseline leads by 1.8 points under partial effectiveness and 6.5 under range restriction.

Research details

Protocol and references

Evaluation protocol

The main pool contains 28 LIBERO tasks, with faults on j₀, j₄, j₅, and j₆ during training. Joint j₂ is excluded from every training stage and task selection. Three seeds train for 1.4 million environment steps each. The six held-out-joint rows use a separate common eight-task subset. Physical joint locks are software enforced at the reference layer.
